About

Data that people make decisions on has to be right.

I spent four years at Commonwealth Care Alliance, a Boston managed care organization, as the engineer on the other end of the pipeline when something looked wrong. That shaped how I work: I care less about how elegant a pipeline looks than about whether the numbers coming out of it reconcile, whether the failure modes are documented, and whether the person who inherits it can understand it without me.

Most of my production work has been data movement: legacy-to-Azure migrations, ETL/ELT builds in Azure Data Factory, Talend and SSIS, EDI X12 and XML exchange frameworks for payers and providers, and REST API integrations that replaced manual file drops. Alongside that, Tier 2/3 support: triaging defects, tracing them through logs, execution plans, and API payloads, and coordinating the fix.

I'm now finishing an M.S. in Artificial Intelligence with a Data Analytics specialization, and pushing into agent-based systems and applied AI, most recently at a Sundai Club hackathon, where I worked on the AI agent layer and simulation logic for a multi-species 3D world. The projects below span both halves: the production data engineering, and the AI and analytics work that came after it.

Data Analysis · Root-Cause Investigation

Claims Processing Pipeline Investigation

Case study · Managed care claims operations

A 136% rise in claim processing time, 6.2 to 14.6 hours average over six months, traced end to end. SQL diagnostics isolated the cause: a broken ICD-10 validation rule pushing Specialty Drug claims (18% of volume) into manual review, consuming 80% of review capacity. The write-up specifies a three-part fix (validation patch, pre-load data quality check, and a monitoring alert) projected to cut processing time 65% and restore an 82% auto-approval rate.

Insight Fusion: Student Performance Dataset

M.S. coursework · Kaggle dataset, n = 395

A correlation and visualization study that pushed back on an intuitive story. Study time barely moves final grades (Pearson r = 0.098; Spearman ρ = 0.105) and absences essentially not at all (r = 0.034), while prior-term grades dominate (G2↔G3 r ≈ 0.905). The analysis covers confounding, reverse causality, and measurement limits, why correlation here isn't a mandate for policy.
