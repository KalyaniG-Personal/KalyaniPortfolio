01Explainable AI & its importance

Powerful isn't the same as trustworthy.

Explainable AI is the set of methods that show people why a model produced an output: what it relied on, how sure it is, and when not to believe it.

Explainability

Why did it give this answer? A reason a person can read and check.

Interpretability

How does it work inside? Mapping internal parts to human concepts.

Healthcare

Clinicians must verify

A model that summarizes a chart or flags a claim has to show which records it used, so a person can catch the error before a patient is affected.

Finance

Decisions need reasons

Credit and fraud decisions must be explainable to the customer affected and to the regulators who audit them.

Law

Sources must be real

A confident answer that cites a case that doesn't exist is worse than no answer. Reasoning has to trace back to real sources.

Everyone

Fix, audit, own it

Explanations help teams find the cause of failures, catch bias, and decide who is accountable when something goes wrong.

02Challenges in explainability

Six reasons the box stays dark.

These problems come from how the models are built, so bigger or better models won't solve them on their own. Open each one to read more.

Model complexity

Knowledge is spread across billions of parameters, and a single neuron often mixes several unrelated concepts. No one part of the model “means” one thing, so you can't just point to where a decision was made.

Post-hoc reasoning

Ask a model “why?” and it writes a plausible story after the fact. That story may not match what actually drove the answer, so a convincing explanation can still be wrong.

Training data

Models learn from internet-scale text and pick up its gaps, noise, and stereotypes. With trillions of words involved, it's very hard to trace which data caused which behavior.

Closed vs. open weights

For closed models like GPT, Claude, and Gemini, only the developer can look inside. Everyone else sees inputs and outputs. Open-weight models like Llama and Gemma can be studied by anyone.

EU AI Act · Art. 50 · in force 2 Aug 2026

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations are now enforceable. Laws require disclosure and documentation, but they don't define how much explanation is “enough” for a given use.

Constant updates

New model versions ship every few months. An explanation method that was checked on one version may not hold for the next, so validation has to be repeated.

03Validation & performance metrics

Explanations say why.
Validation proves it works.

Validation runs through a model's whole life, from its training data to its use in production. Click a stage to see what it checks.

Data validation

Before training, check that the data is accurate, representative of the people the model will serve, and audited for bias and duplicates.

“Is the model learning from the right examples?”

Six metrics, six plain questions

Each metric answers something a manager or patient would actually ask. Hover over a card to see how it's measured.

Accuracy · F1

How often is it right?

Compare its answers with expert-labeled answers on data it hasn't seen.

Calibration · ECE

When it says “90% sure,” is it right 90% of the time?

Expected calibration error compares stated confidence with real accuracy.

Groundedness

Does it make things up?

Hallucination rate: each claim is checked against the source documents.

Robustness

Does rewording the question change the answer?

Consistency under paraphrases, typos, and adversarial inputs.

Fairness

Is it equally good for everyone?

Performance gaps across demographic groups and customer segments.

★ Explanation faithfulness

Does the stated reason match what really drove the answer?

Remove or change the cited evidence. If the answer doesn't change, the explanation wasn't the real reason.

NIST AI RMFNIST's AI Risk Management Framework lists “valid and reliable” first among its characteristics of trustworthy AI, alongside “explainable and interpretable.” Validation is the base that the other characteristics rest on.

04Current solutions & techniques

Going deeper
into the model.

Explainability methods range from surface-level, where you only see inputs and outputs, to deep, where you inspect the model's internal workings. The deeper you go, the closer you get to the truth, and the more expertise and access it takes.

SurfaceInsideMechanism
Depth 1 · outside the model

Chain-of-thought

The model writes out its steps before answering, so a person or a second “monitor” model can read them and flag problems.

Built into reasoning models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

Depth 2 · outside the model

Feature attribution

SHAP- and LIME-style methods score how much each input word changed the output. This is the highlighting in the demo at the top of the page.

Example from the demo above: “no prior authorization” gets the highest score.

Depth 3 · inside the model

Attention & probing

Visualize which words the model pays attention to, and train small “probe” classifiers to test whether a concept is stored in a given layer.

A useful first look, but attention alone isn't a full explanation.

Depth 4 · the mechanism

Features & circuits

Sparse autoencoders split the model's internal signals into readable concepts. Attribution graphs then trace how those concepts combine, step by step, into an answer.

Has shown a model planning a rhyme before it writes the line.

At every depth: model cards and system cards publish what was tested, how, and with what results. They let outsiders judge a model they can't look inside.

05Industry efforts

Four labs, four bets.

Each leading lab focuses on a different depth of the toolkit, and more of the tools are now released openly.

The bet: read the model's internals directly, and turn them into language people can understand.

Models: Claude

May 2025 · open source

Circuit tracing

Tools that generate attribution graphs showing the steps a model took internally to reach an output. Anyone can explore them on Neuronpedia using open-weight models.

May 2026

Natural Language Autoencoders

Translate the model's internal activations into plain-English text a reviewer can read. Used to audit models for hidden motivations before release.

The bet: keep the reasoning readable, and measure whether it stays that way as models scale.

Models: GPT · o-series reasoning models

Dec 2025

Chain-of-thought monitorability

A formal evaluation suite of 13 evaluations across 24 environments that tests whether a model's reasoning can be monitored for misbehavior.

2026

CoT-Control

Found that current reasoning models struggle to deliberately hide their reasoning. OpenAI treats this as one safety layer, not a lie detector.

The bet: open interpretability tools at scale, as shared infrastructure for everyone.

Models: Gemini · Gemma

Dec 2025 · open source

Gemma Scope 2

Sparse autoencoders and transcoders for every layer of all Gemma 3 models, from 270M to 27B parameters, built from about 110 petabytes of data.

Why it matters

Root-cause debugging

Researchers can trace hallucinations, jailbreaks, and unsafe reasoning back to specific internal circuits instead of only patching the outputs.

The bet: open weights and open evaluations, so anyone can inspect and test the model.

Models: Llama

Since Dec 2023

Purple Llama

Llama Guard safety classifiers screen inputs and outputs. The CyberSecEval benchmarks measure cybersecurity risks, and CyberSecEval 3 covers eight of them.

Open weights

Research by anyone

Because Llama's weights are public, outside researchers can look inside. Anthropic's circuit tracer already supports Llama 3.2-1B.

Common groundIn July 2025, researchers from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and others co-authored a paper urging every lab to keep model reasoning monitorable and to measure it.

07Key takeaways

What to remember.

01

Opacity is built in

LLMs spread decisions across billions of parameters, and their own explanations can be unfaithful. Explainability has to be engineered on purpose.

02

The tools are maturing

From chain-of-thought monitoring to circuit tracing, labs are moving from guessing toward direct inspection, and releasing more of it openly.

03

Explain + validate + measure

No single method is enough. Trust comes from explanations people can check, validation for the specific use, and metrics tracked over time.
