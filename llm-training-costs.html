The Cost of Training an LLM

How models like GPT-4, Gemini, Llama, Claude and DeepSeek go from raw text to a deployed assistant, and what each stage consumes in data, compute, energy, time and people.

1 · The training process

Eight steps from raw text to a deployed model

An LLM learns by predicting the next token in enormous amounts of text9,22. That raw predictor is then tuned to follow instructions and behave safely before it is released. Each step draws on a different mix of resources.

Where the GPU-hours go: DeepSeek-V3, the most detailed public breakdown

DeepSeek published GPU-hours for each stage of its final training run4. Pre-training used 96% of the compute; post-training used well under 1%.

Pre-training 2,664K GPU-h · 95.6% Context extension 119K · 4.3% Post-training 5K · 0.2%

Total 2.788M H800 GPU-hours. GPU-hours leave out the cost of human labelers, which is where post-training spends most of its money. Reasoning models released since 2024 spend far more compute on reinforcement learning after pre-training.

2 · The resources

Four resources set the cost, and people set the budget

Scaling research shows that a model improves predictably as parameters, data and compute grow together8. That relationship is why each resource below has climbed with every model generation.

Data

What it is
Trillions of tokens of text and code, plus human-written examples and rankings for post-training.
Example
Llama 3.1: 15T+ tokens. DeepSeek-V3: 14.8T tokens.
Cost
Rising. Google pays Reddit about $60M a year for access5. Anthropic’s $1.5B authors’ settlement priced unlicensed books at about $3,000 each10.
Effect on performance
More and cleaner data lifts quality at a fixed model size.

Compute

What it is
GPU or TPU clusters linked by fast networks, measured in GPU-hours and FLOP.
Example
Llama 3.1 405B: 3.8 × 10²⁵ FLOP on up to 16,384 H100s12.
Cost
The largest line item. Hardware is 47–67% of total development cost3.
Effect on performance
The strongest single predictor of capability across model generations.

Energy

What it is
Electricity for the chips plus cooling and data center overhead.
Example
Llama 3.1 405B GPUs alone: about 21.6 GWh*. Gemini 1.0 Ultra drew an estimated 35 MW3.
Cost
Only 2–6% of dollars3, but power capacity decides where and how fast labs can train.
Effect on performance
Indirect. Available power caps the size of the cluster.

Time

What it is
Months of wall-clock training, plus months of data work, tuning and safety testing.
Example
DeepSeek-V3: under 2 months on 2,048 GPUs. Llama 3: 419 unplanned interruptions in a 54-day stretch18.
Cost
Every day of a run rents or ties up the whole cluster, and staff are 29–49% of cost3.
Effect on performance
Longer runs see more data; failures and restarts waste it.

* My calculation: 30.84M GPU-hours × 700 W per H100 = 21.6 GWh, roughly the yearly electricity use of 2,000 U.S. homes. It counts the GPUs only, not cooling or other servers.

3 · The money and the footprint

What training runs cost, and where the money goes

Few labs publish costs. The figures below are independent estimates or developer statements, and they measure different things, so compare them with care.

Estimated compute cost of the final training run

US dollars, millions. Hover a bar for its source and method.

Gemini, GPT-4, Llama and Grok: Stanford AI Index estimates of compute cost19,20. Claude 3.5 Sonnet: “a few $10M’s”, according to Anthropic’s CEO1; the dashed band shows that range, not an exact figure. DeepSeek-V3: the developer’s own figure at $2 per GPU-hour, final run only4. None of these include research experiments, failed runs, staff or buying the hardware. OpenAI’s CEO put GPT-4’s total cost at more than $100M11.

Share of total development cost

Range across GPT-3, OPT-175B, GPT-4 and Gemini 1.0 Ultra3.

Estimated training emissions

Tonnes of CO₂-equivalent.

GPT-3: Patterson et al.17. GPT-4 and Grok 4: AI Index 2026 estimates, which its editors say to read with caution; Epoch AI puts Grok 4 near 140,000 t21. Llama: Meta’s location-based figure (zero market-based, because of renewable energy purchases)13.

5 · What this means

Key takeaways

  • Pre-training dominates compute. It used 96% of DeepSeek-V3’s GPU-hours. Post-training costs less in compute but more in human labor.
  • Hardware and people dominate dollars, not electricity. Energy is 2–6% of cost but limits where and how big a cluster can be.
  • A headline cost is only part of the bill. A final-run figure leaves out experiments, failed runs, staff, data licensing and the hardware itself. DeepSeek’s $5.6M and Gemini Ultra’s $191M are not measured the same way.
  • Efficiency competes with scale. DeepSeek-V3 activates only 37B of its 671B parameters per token, which cuts training compute for its size.
  • Data is becoming a direct cost. Licensing deals and copyright settlements now put a price on training text.
  • Costs keep rising. Frontier training cost has grown about 2.4× a year, and Grok 4’s estimated emissions are roughly 8× Llama 3.1 405B’s.
